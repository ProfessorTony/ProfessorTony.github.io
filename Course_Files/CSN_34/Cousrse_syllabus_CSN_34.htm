💻 Course Description: Your Mission, Should You Choose to Accept It...

Welcome to **Computer Forensics**! Ever wondered what real-world challenges exist in the connected world? The study of Computer Forensics will open your eyes. Forensics Computing, Digital Forensics, or Computer Forensics is a newly emerging field of study and practice that incorporates many areas of expertise. We will explore network security, intrusion detection, incident response, infrastructure protection, disaster recovery, continuity planning, cybersecurity, and computer crime investigation. Be Cyber Aware!

Official Course Description:

     This is an introductory course in Computer Forensics. Forensics Computing, Digital Forensics, or Computer Forensics is the name of a newly emerging field of study and practice that incorporates many areas of expertise. Some of these areas include network security, intrusion detection, incident response, infrastructure protection, disaster recovery, continuity planning, software engineering, cybersecurity, and computer crime investigation. It is an area of practice in public law enforcement at the federal, state, and local levels that deals with cybercrime, cyber vandalism, cyber predators, and cyberterrorism. In the private sector, it involves critical infrastructure, including businesses, hospitals, utilities, transportation, finance, education, and other key institutions). (CSU)

🎯 Course Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

  • Students will master industry-standard forensic software tools such as EnCase, FTK, Autopsy, and SANS SIFT, while understanding tool limitations, validation procedures, and when to apply specific tools for different investigative scenarios.
  • Students will develop skills in analyzing network traffic, examining mobile devices and cloud storage, investigating internet artifacts, and understanding modern digital communication channels and their forensic implications.
  • Students will gain proficiency in examining various file systems (NTFS, FAT, ext4, HFS+), recovering deleted files, analyzing file metadata, and understanding data storage structures across different operating systems.
  • Students will learn proper techniques for collecting and preserving digital evidence while maintaining chain of custody protocols and ensuring forensic integrity.
  • Students will understand systematic approaches to computer forensic investigations, including case documentation, evidence analysis workflows, and adherence to legal and ethical standards throughout the investigative process.

💡 Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

  • ** Subject matter knowledge will be measured by demonstrating fundamental knowledge of Management Information Systems (M.I.S.) and its terminology. **
  • Develop and test hypotheses based on digital evidence analysis.
  • Prepare comprehensive forensic reports that clearly communicate technical findings to non-technical audiences.
  • Operate professional forensic software suites such as EnCase, FTK, or open-source alternatives like Autopsy.
  • Identify and categorize different types of digital evidence found on computers, mobile devices, and network systems.

🪑“Excessive Absenteeism” Policy!

“Excessive absenteeism” in a course is defined as not participating in the course for an adequate amount of time to successfully pass the course or adequately complete the course. Participation in the course involves completing course activities – simply logging in does not count as participation. If a student does not submit assignments during a time period of 8 consecutive days, 2 or more assignments, OR if a student does not complete a minimum of 65% of assignments by the drop date, the student runs the risk of being dropped from the course for excessive absenteeism.
